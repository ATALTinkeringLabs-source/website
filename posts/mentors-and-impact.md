Mentors play a crucial role in creating a safe environment for experimentation. They guide students without overshadowing their own thinking, encouraging them to explore, reflect, and improve their ideas.

Good mentorship helps young innovators connect classroom learning to real-life challenges. It teaches them to ask better questions, develop a process, and value iteration over perfection.

When students gain guidance and encouragement, they become more confident in presenting their work and more committed to solving meaningful problems.

The result is not just a project but a mindset: students who can think critically, build creatively, and act responsibly.
