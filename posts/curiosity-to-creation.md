Curiosity is often the first spark behind every major innovation. Students do not begin with perfect models or polished plans; they begin by asking better questions and experimenting without fear.

Making something tangible allows a learner to look at an idea from multiple angles. They can identify what works, what fails, and what needs to change in the next iteration.

This process builds not only technical skills but also resilience. Students learn that failure is not the end of a project; it is feedback that improves the next version.

Confidence grows when a learner sees an idea move from sketch to prototype to meaningful real-world use.
