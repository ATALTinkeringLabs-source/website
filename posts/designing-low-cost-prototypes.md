At the heart of any meaningful innovation is a real problem. In ATL spaces, students begin by observing their surroundings, interviewing users, and noticing where everyday systems fall short.

Rather than jumping to solutions immediately, learners spend time understanding patterns, needs, and constraints. This approach makes the final prototype more practical and impactful.

Low-cost prototyping gives students the freedom to test ideas quickly. Whether it is a sensor, a model, or a simple tool, the goal is to learn through iteration and feedback.

By combining empathy with experimentation, teams build confidence and create solutions that are useful for their communities rather than theoretical alone.
