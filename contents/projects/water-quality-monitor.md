After visiting a local river, students became concerned about water quality. They partnered with a local environmental organization to understand what data would be most useful for monitoring.

The team built a handheld device using affordable sensors that measure pH, dissolved oxygen, and turbidity. The data is logged to a cloud system for easy tracking over time.

Through field testing, they learned how to calibrate sensors, handle data errors, and communicate findings to non-technical audiences. Their device has helped identify pollution hotspots that local authorities are now investigating.

This project expanded into a community citizen science program, demonstrating how student innovation can create real environmental impact.
